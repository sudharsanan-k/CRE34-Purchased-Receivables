CRE34.1 – CRE34.12 • Dual-Risk IRB Framework

Purchased Receivables: Default Risk & Dilution Risk

Purchased receivables require two separate IRB unexpected loss capital charges. First, the obligor can fail to pay due to insolvency (Default Risk). Second, non-cash credits such as product returns, quality disputes, and early-payment rebates can reduce the invoice balance (Dilution Risk). Banks must calculate both loss estimates on a strict stand-alone basis. Seller recourse and external guarantees are excluded from pool parameter estimation.

Concrete Banking Example: A bank buys a $100.00M pool of corporate trade invoices and grants a $50.00M revolving purchase line. Under CRE34.5, the 40% CCF produces $120.00M in Gross Exposure. The bank calculates $4.97M in Dilution Capital first, deducts $4.97M to get $115.03M in Net Default EAD, and then computes Default RWA.
Benchmark Calculation ($120M Pool) F-IRB Senior
1. Gross Pool ($100M + 40%×$50M): $120.00M
2. K_Dilution (EL 0.40%, LGD 100%, 1y): $4.97M (RWA $62.08M)
3. Net Default EAD ($120M − $4.97M): $115.03M
4. Default (EL 0.60% → PD 1.50%, LGD 40%): RW 65.17% → $74.97M
5. Combined RWA (8% UL Capital): $137.05M ($10.96M)

Mandatory CRE34 Calculation Pipeline

Sequential order prevents double-counting of dilution losses
STAGE 1 • EXPOSURE
Gross Receivable Pool

Sum drawn receivables plus a fixed 40% CCF on undrawn revolving purchase commitments (even under A-IRB).

STAGE 2 • CRE34.8–34.9
Compute K_Dilution

Apply Corporate IRB formula on Gross Exposure with PD = EL_Dilution and LGD = 100% for both retail and corporate pools.

STAGE 3 • CRE34.5–34.6
Net Default EAD

Subtract pre-mitigation K_Dilution from Gross Exposure to calculate the stand-alone Net Default EAD.

STAGE 4 • CRE34.2–34.7
Compute Default RWA

Evaluate Net Default EAD via Retail/QRRE, Corporate F-IRB (40% or 100% LGD), A-IRB, or Hybrid highest-RW rules.

STAGE 5 • CRE34.10–34.12
PPD & CRM Substitution

Route refundable PPD to CRE40–45 securitisation rules and substitute eligible guarantor RW across covered risks.
